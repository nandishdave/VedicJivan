Reading a whole life from a chart

The Balanced-Life score — a classical four-fold reading of thirteen life areas, and an honest account of what it can and cannot tell you.

Companion to the fame study (“Can a chart tell famous from ordinary?”). Unlike that one, this method is not statistically validated — read the limits at the end before trusting a number.

Two different questions, two separate axes

A chart can be read for very different things, and they don’t move together. We keep two apart:

Worldly-Prominence

Fame, recognition, worldly reach. Built from a cross-validated 17-factor model (the fame study). Robust, tested against 321 charts.

Balanced-Life

Whether a life is whole across its areas — health, marriage, children, parents, wealth, work. A classical reading. This page.

They are near-independent: a chart can be worldly-strong yet uneven, or ordinary yet beautifully rounded. That is why they’re shown as two separate numbers, never merged.

The four-fold method — how each life area is judged

Every life area is scored the way an astrologer reads a bhāva: not from one signal but from four, each a 0–100 sub-score, combined by weight.

FactorWeightWhat raises it / lowers it
Bhāveśa
house-lord		30% The lord’s dignity (exalted 100 … debilitated 5) and placement (trikoṇa +12, kendra +8, dusthāna −22), plus a Shadbala nudge. Often the single most telling factor for a house.
Bhāva
the house		28% The house’s own Ashtakavarga bindus (40+ high, ~28 average), benefic vs malefic occupants and aspects on it.
Varga
divisional		30% The area’s own divisional chart judged as a chart — its lagna-lord, its relevant house (e.g. the 5th-of-D7 for children, with occupants), and the kāraka’s dignity, all inside the varga.
Kāraka
significator		12% The natural significator’s Shadbala — a light modifier (it’s the same planet regardless of which chart, so it can’t dominate).

Pāpa / Śubha Kartari — hemming

A house (or its lord) caught between two malefics is hemmed — −12 (Pāpa Kartari); between two benefics, +8 (Śubha Kartari). Applied to both the Bhāva and the Bhāveśa.

The single number, and why it’s soft

The aggregate Balanced-Life % is a weakest-links blend of the thirteen — 0.45·mean + 0.35·(weakest three) + 0.20·minimum — so one empty area drags it down and a great career can’t hide a null marriage. But honestly: averaging thirteen near-independent areas pulls almost everyone toward the middle. Across 96 control charts it clustered at ~50 (“Mixed”). Treat the single % as a soft summary; the per-area numbers carry the real signal.

⚠ Birth time is everything here

Every area is read from the ascendant, which turns a full sign every ~2 hours. On an unverified birth time the reading is close to noise. In one test, a public figure’s “Children” area swung from 32 to 63 across the day — the placeholder time happened to land on the worst value. These readings are only meaningful on an accurate, recorded birth time.

What this is — and what it is not

Not validated. Unlike the fame model (cross-validated, AUC ≈ 0.77), this is classical judgment. We have no ground-truth dataset of who had a good marriage or long life, so we cannot claim it predicts outcomes. Diagnostics on 96 charts show it behaves sensibly — each area discriminates, the areas are largely independent, the engine isn’t broken — but sensible behaviour is not proof of correctness.

A weak area is a tendency, not a verdict. “Weak Children” means the classical strength of that area reads low — difficulty, worry, delay, health of children — not a yes/no event. People with a weak 5th house have three children all the time.

No third eye. For those reasons we deliberately never tell a reader “you will struggle in marriage / children / health.” We show the transparent per-area numbers and let them interpret — a guide, held humbly, not a prophecy.

Method: Whole-Sign houses, Lahiri ayanāṁśa (Swiss Ephemeris). All weights and thresholds are deliberate, tunable judgment calls. This score is a classical reading offered as guidance — for any real decision, consult a qualified astrologer.
